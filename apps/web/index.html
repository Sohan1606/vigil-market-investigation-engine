01 — THE PROBLEM

Prediction is easy.
Knowing when to trust it is hard.

Any model can emit a number. The expensive question is whether that number deserves a decision — after costs, after uncertainty, after the possibility that the market has simply changed character since the model was fit.

VIGIL is built around that question. Every forecast arrives with its evidence, its uncertainty, its failure modes and a verdict that is allowed to be “not enough”.

02 — OBSERVE

What is happening?

A real pipeline, not a CSV: NSE sessions land in a partitioned lake, pass a data contract, and stream through bounded-memory sketches — Bloom, Flajolet-Martin, DGIM — that watch an unbounded tape of market events with kilobytes of state.

03 — INVESTIGATE

Why is it happening?

Unusual activity opens a case. The case carries an evidence ledger: what supports the reading, what contradicts it, what was simply unavailable — each item timestamped and weighted, each conclusion phrased as temporal association, never as proven cause.

04 — FORECAST

What could happen next?

Six model families compete on identical walk-forward folds — baseline, logistic regression, random forest, gradient boosting, an LSTM and a temporal-attention benchmark. They are ranked by probability quality, not by the accuracy that flatters them.

05 — CHALLENGE

What would make this wrong?

Before any verdict, the forecast is stress-tested: volatility doubles, a model drops out, the news channel goes dark, data arrives late. If the conclusion cannot survive the stress set, VIGIL says so in the verdict instead of burying it in a footnote.

06 — VERDICT

Sometimes the most intelligent signal is NO ACTION.

Nine gate checks — data quality, conviction, agreement, uncertainty, calibration, model health, market stability, risk and cost advantage. A forecast becomes a bias only when the expected edge survives a realistic round-trip cost. Otherwise it is logged, not traded.

07 — REPLAY

Would VIGIL have known this then?

Pick a historical session. VIGIL retrains on data strictly older than that day, hides every headline published after the cutoff, and reconstructs the decision it would have made — then grades it against what actually happened.

08 — RESEARCH ENGINE

Does the system actually work?

Five experiments, one protocol, published as measured. Some hypotheses are NOT SUPPORTED — including ours. Daily direction is close to a coin flip, and after costs most signals lose. That result is in the product, not hidden behind a demo.
